Current personal finances improved for low-wealth consumers and, to a lesser extent, middle-wealth consumers, who experienced some relief with falling gas prices. In contrast, those with the largest stockholdings reported a decline in personal finances, consistent with continued AI-related agitation in stock markets. Consumers appear to anticipate potential changes to public policy under a divided government to come. Accordingly, the expectations index surged for independents, who swung to the left in the midterms, and increased more moderately for Democrats. Expectations fell for Republicans, who may not favor a slowdown of the White House policy agenda. Sentiment remains subdued at 9% below February, prior to the start of the Iran conflict, reflecting continued uncertainty regarding the cost-of-living factors that dominate consumers’ perspectives. This release covers interviews completed between October 20th and November 16th. Median year-ahead inflation expectations plunged from 4.7% last month to 3.7% this month. Still, it remains meaningfully above the 3.4% seen in February before the Iran conflict began, along with all 2024 readings. Long-run inflation expectations ticked down from 3.5% in October to 3.3%, higher than their 2024 range of 2.8% to 3.2%. Despite the softening in inflation expectations, consumers continue to report overwhelmingly that inflation—rather than unemployment—will cause greater hardship for consumers in the next one or two years.
