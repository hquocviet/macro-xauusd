Modest improvements in multiple domains, including expected personal finances and business conditions, were concentrated primarily among independents. While sentiment lifted for lower- and middle-wealth consumers, it fell 5% for those with the largest stock portfolios in the wake of a relatively turbulent period in stock markets. Moreover, consumers’ comments revealed growing anxiety over a possible AI bubble. AI worries, however, were dwarfed by cost of living concerns, which continue to be at the forefront of consumers’ minds across the population. About 57% spontaneously mentioned that their living standards have been eroded by high prices, the highest seen since May and up from 46% in February, just prior to the Iran conflict. Despite this month’s improvement, sentiment is down 10% from February. Interviews for this release were conducted between October 20 and November 2. In the two months leading into the election, more than two-thirds of consumers expected Democrats to win the House, and over half anticipated the same for the Senate (see the October 30th special report on election expectations ). Year-ahead inflation expectations declined from 4.7% last month to 4.3% this month. Although the current reading is the lowest since August, it remains substantially above the 3.4% seen in February before the Iran conflict began, along with all 2024 readings.
